### E3 — C2 is a single-experiment check. 737 of 972 homologous series are skipped, and 441 of them for no stated reason.

**File.** `provenance/audit.py`, `c2_monotonic()`, line 117: `NAMING_IS_LENGTH = {"KW_2014"}`.

This is the "737 series skipped" line in the printed report, and the skip rule is not sound.

**What the skip actually is.** The function first applies a CH2-step gate: consecutive mass steps must be positive whole multiples of 14.0157 in ≥70% of steps. **972 vitro series pass that gate.** Then line 133 discards every series whose `Experiment_ID` is not `KW_2014` — 737 of them. So 235 of 972 are checked, and the check is effectively `KW_2014`-only.

**The gate already does the job the whitelist claims to need.** If a series' consecutive mass steps are integer CH2 multiples across the series, the numeric token behaves as a chain length *by construction* — that is what the gate measures. The whitelist is redundant and it suppresses 76% of the coverage.

**Evidence from the skipped set.** These all passed the CH2 gate and are unambiguously homologous (steps shown as multiples of CH2 per index step):

|---|---|---|---|

| JM_2016 | `ZA1-Ep*` | Ep8…Ep18 | 2.0 ×5 |
| LL_2026 | `8A1-1B-EP*` | EP6…EP18 | 4.0 ×6 |

The code comment names three exclusions — `KS_2024` `C24` (two C4 tails), `KZ_2016` `SC1/SC2/SC3` (tail identifiers), `YX_2024` `C1/C2/C3` (component indices). The printed line "token is not a chain length" asserts something false about 441 of the 737 series it covers.

**Mutation evidence.** Making a mass non-monotonic in `AA_2008 87_O18` (−200 Da), `LL_2026 8A1-1B-EP18` (−400 Da) and `QL_2026 70-L1-C14` (−100 Da) produced `violations: 0` in all three cases. **Correct fix, prototyped and measured.** Derive the convention per `(Experiment_ID, token_prefix)` instead of whitelisting one experiment: accept a group if it has ≥3 series and ≥90% of them are *fully* CH2-homologous, then assert monotonicity on every series in the group. I implemented it and ran it:

- **676 series checked**, up from 235 (2.9×), across **18** `(experiment, token)` groups instead of 1;
- **0 violations on the clean corpus** — it ships green;
- it **automatically rejects** every group the comment names: `KS_2024` `C`, `KZ_2016` `SC`/`SN`/`SO`, `YX_2024` `C`, `BL_2023` `T`, `BL_2023_3CR` `T`, `BL_2024`, `FG_2026`, `JC_2023` `C`, `JL_2024` `B`, `JW_2024` `R`, `NC_2024` `X`, `XH_2025` `D` — each at 0% fully-homologous, so the rejection is derived, not asserted;
- it **fires on all five** mutations that current C2 misses or catches, including `A376_C14` and `87_O18`.

**SEVERITY:** changes values — a mass or structure error inside a homologous series is invisible in 76% of the corpus's homologous series. **CONFIDENCE: certain.**

### E4 — C2's 0.7 gate is self-defeating: the worse the corruption, the more likely it escapes

**File.** `provenance/audit.py`, line 131: `if good < 0.7 * len(steps): continue`.

`good` counts steps that are *positive* CH2 multiples. A violation is a non-positive step. So a violation reduces `good`, and enough violations push the series below the gate — at which point it is dropped from the checked set instead of being reported.

**Evidence.** Measured on `KW_2014`, whose series-size distribution is `{2: 1, 4: 2, 5: 233}`:

| mutation | steps | good | gate `0.7·n` | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one violation in the 4-member `A376_C` series | 3 | 2 | 2.1 | **dropped**, `series_checked 235→234`, `violations 0` |
| one violation in the 5-member `A125_C` series | 4 | 3 | 2.8 | reported, `violations 1` |
| **two** violations in the same 5-member series | 4 | 2 | 2.8 | **dropped**, `series_checked 235→234`, `violations 0` |

The only trace is `series_checked` falling by 1 — a number printed as "235 verified-length series checked" that nobody is watching, and which also moves whenever the corpus legitimately grows. A 3-member series (the minimum `len(u) >= 3` admits) can never report a violation at all: one bad step out of two gives `good = 1 < 1.4`. `KW_2014` happens to have no 3-member series today, so this is latent rather than active, but it will not stay that way as papers merge.

**Correct value.** Separate the two questions. Decide whether the token is a chain length from the *magnitudes* of the steps (`|step| / CH2` near an integer), independently of sign; then assert positivity. The prototype in E3 does this and catches the 4-member case.

**SEVERITY:** changes values. **CONFIDENCE: certain.**

### E9 — two of the 14 C10 anchors are byte-identical to the enumerator's own output, and their notes claim they are not

This is the failure mode the ledger explicitly warns about: *"a C10 anchor copied from the enumeration (can only pass)"*.

**Method.** The ledger's own objection is that string comparison cannot settle this, because a canonicalised honest derivation is byte-identical to a copy. True — so I used a different discriminator: **what the repository's own convention is.** For each anchor I compared the stored `reference_SMILES` to the enumerated `IL_SMILES` four ways (byte-equal; equal to `MolToSmiles(enumerated)`; skeleton-equal; and whether the enumerated string is itself canonical), and I looked for any code or intermediate artifact that builds a reference.

| paper | anchor | ref == enum, byte | ref == canon(enum) | skeleton match | reference-building code present | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lin_aromatic_2026 | `10A2-3B-EP10` | **TRUE** | **TRUE** | yes | **none** | **cannot be evidenced as independent** |
| siegwart_42634137 | `C6O2B2` | **TRUE** | **TRUE** | yes | **none** | **cannot be evidenced as independent** |
| ql_2026 | `T3A-L5-C10` | false | false | yes | none, but string is non-canonical and amine-rooted | independent — a copy could not produce it |
| wht_2026_falcon | `DLin-MC3-DMA` | false | false | yes | none, non-canonical | independent |
| wht_2026_falcon | `SM-102` | false | false | yes | none, non-canonical | independent |
| xd_2026_lipidai | `SM-102` | false | false | yes | none, head-first ordering | independent |
| xd_2026_lipidai | `DLin-MC3-DMA` | false | false | yes | none, amine-first ordering | independent |
| xd_2026_lipidai | `ALC-0315` | false | false | **no** (`expected_mismatch`) | none | independent — a copy could not disagree |
| xd_2026_lipidai | `E1`–`E9` (9) | — | — | — | — | `NO_DRAWN_STRUCTURE`, correctly empty |
| ng_2025 | 6 anchors | not assessed | | | `stage08_reference.py` **exists** | excluded on instruction |

Every enumerated string in the corpus is already canonical (`enum == canon(enum)` is TRUE for all eight). So the repository's convention is visible: six of the eight anchors store a **hand-typed, non-canonical** string, in a deliberately different atom order, and their notes say so ("written head-first", "a deliberately different atom order from the corpus string", "amine-first"). Two store exactly the canonical form. That is the anomaly.

**What makes it a finding rather than a quibble.** Both of those two anchors carry a note asserting the opposite:

- `lin_aromatic_2026`: *"Assembled from fragments independently of enumerate_library.py."*
- `siegwart_42634137`: *"Connectivity read off the drawn structure and assembled from fragments, independently of enumerate.py."*

and `CLAUDE.md` line 369 states it as established fact: *"Anchors created for both curated papers — Siegwart C6O2B2 (Fig 2a) and Lin 10A2-3B-EP10 (Fig S17 …) — both exact matches, **each assembled by a different code path than the enumerator**."* There is no artifact of that different code path anywhere in either folder: no script, no intermediate CSV, no fragment file. `grep -rl reference_structures` over the repository returns a reference-building script in exactly one folder — `curation/ng_2025/stage08_reference.py` — which is the correct pattern and the only instance of it.

**The honest reading, both ways.** There *is* positive evidence that an independent assembly happened at creation time for Siegwart: `CHANGELOG.md` line 241 records that building the anchor corrected C6O2B2's formula (`C117H217N5O12+ → C117H218N5O12+`, MW 1886.05 → 1887.05), and CLAUDE.md records that it caught a SMILES ring-closure collision that had fused the piperazine to cholesterol. A copied reference could not have found either. So the claim is probably true *as history*.

But that is not what the anchor does now. Once the enumerator was corrected to agree and the reference stored as the canonical string, the artifact stopped being an independent derivation and became a **regression lock**: it detects future drift in the enumerator (my C10a mutation proves it does, and well) but it can no longer testify that today's structure is right. For `lin_aromatic_2026` that distinction bites hardest, because the class the anchor is supposed to cover for that paper is **class 15, regiochemistry** — and a reference that is byte-identical to the enumeration has exactly zero discriminating power on which nitrogen carries which arm.

**Correct value.** Two things, neither expensive:
1. Re-derive those two references now, by fragment assembly, and store them in a non-canonical atom order like the other six. If they agree, the anchors become evidence again at the cost of one session.
2. Add an advisory status to `structure_check.py` — `ANCHOR_BYTE_IDENTICAL_TO_ENUMERATION` — that fires whenever `reference_SMILES == enumerated IL_SMILES` verbatim. The ledger is right that this cannot *prove* copying, so it must not fail the build; but as a visible flag requiring a `read_by` note that names the code path, it converts an invisible risk into a reviewable one. Under the repository's actual convention it would currently flag exactly these two rows and no others.

**How to verify.** After re-derivation, confirm `ref != enum` byte-wise and `skeleton_match == True`; and confirm the advisory status fires on a deliberately pasted reference.

**SEVERITY:** changes structures — not the data, but the assurance the project believes it has on two of the three papers whose structural bugs created C10. **CONFIDENCE: probable.** Byte-identity is not proof, and I say so; what is certain is that no artifact in the repository can evidence the independence that two notes and CLAUDE.md assert, and that the convention followed by the other six anchors was not followed by these two.

Thirteen registry entries would close it, and `DODAP` plus `DMPC` are two more that sit immediately beside already-registered members of their own class.

**Correct value.** Add 19 entries, written from the chemical name with the authority recorded, exactly as the existing 13 are. Add `EggSM` to a new `DELIBERATELY_UNREGISTERED` dict with the reason ("natural homolog distribution"), alongside the PEG rule, so the exclusions are enumerated rather than implied by absence — and add a check that every component name in the corpus is in `REGISTRY` **or** in `DELIBERATELY_UNREGISTERED`, so a new unregistered reagent cannot arrive silently. That last check is the ratchet for this class, and it is the piece that is missing.

**How to verify.** My mutation `C11c` — corrupting `DSPE-PEG2000`'s SMILES — produced `rows_mismatched 0`, exit 0. Repeat it against `DODAP` after registration and confirm it fires.

**SEVERITY:** changes structures (potential). **CONFIDENCE: certain.**

### E17 — C11 only checks name → structure, never structure → name

`reagent_registry.py` iterates registered names and checks the structures found under them. The inverse — a *registered structure* appearing under an unregistered or different name — is never checked. The `AC_2025` bug would have been caught either way, but a mislabelled single reagent (MC3's structure stored as `Lipid-47`) only shows up in the inverse direction.

**Evidence.** I implemented the inverse scan — skeleton-match every `{IL,HL,CHL,PEG,fifthcomponent}_SMILES` in both corpus files against the 13 registry skeletons, and report any whose name is not the registered one — and found **0 violations** over the whole corpus. So the check can be added green, today, at no cost.

**Correct value.** Add the inverse pass to `reagent_registry.py` and report `registered_structure_under_wrong_name`.

**SEVERITY:** cosmetic today. **CONFIDENCE: certain.**

**New classes this pass found that are not in the ledger**, each with a check:

1. **A check with no failure path** (E1, E2, E7, E14). Meta-class, and the one that matters most: a check that cannot fail is indistinguishable from an absent check in every report. *Check:* a committed baseline JSON of every counter in `audit_findings.json` and `dataset_validation.json`, asserted on every run, with the suite exiting non-zero on any increase. **On the stopping rule.** CLAUDE.md §6 and REVIEW_PROGRAM.md §6 both say: stop when every known error class has an automated check *and* one adversarial pass finds nothing outside those classes. This pass found five classes outside the ledger, and — more to the point — found that the ledger's 23 "covered" rows overstate coverage in nine places. The ratchet has not slipped in the sense of a check that stopped running; every check still runs and 30 of 43 seeded mutations were detected.
