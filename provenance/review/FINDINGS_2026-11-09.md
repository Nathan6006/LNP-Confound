## F-049 — `JW_2024`'s PEG-lipid name and structure disagree, and the corpus cannot say which is wrong

**Found by:** C17 (`check_reagent_crosscheck.py`) on its first run.
**Status:** OPEN. Baselined in `reagent_crosscheck_baseline.json` with this
reason, so the gate is green on the known condition and fires on any new one.

`JW_2024` (1,873 rows, PMID 37985700) is the **only** experiment in the corpus
that calls its PEG-lipid `DMPE-PEG2000`. The SMILES it carries is byte-identical
to the string that **41 other experiments call `DMG-PEG2000`**, and that
`BL_2023_3CR` calls `C14-PEG2000`.

| side | formula | head group |
|---|---|---|
| `JW_2024`, as `DMPE-PEG2000` | **C122H242O50** | none — a dimyristoyl **glycerol** PEG |
| 8 experiments, as `DMPE-PEG2000`… | **C122H242NO53P** | phosphoethanolamine |

A real DMPE-PEG carries a phosphoethanolamine head. `JW_2024`'s string has **no
nitrogen and no phosphorus at all**. Both sides are C122H242, which is why no
descriptor, mass or composition check has ever flagged it.

**Which half is wrong is undecided, and that is the point.** 41-to-1 says the
structure is the corpus-standard DMG-PEG string, but a majority is not evidence
about what one lab formulated. If the paper used DMG-PEG2000 then only the NAME
is wrong on 1,873 rows and no measured value sits on the wrong molecule; if it
used DMPE-PEG2000 then the STRUCTURE is wrong on 1,873 rows. **This is the
`AC_2025` question** — there the names were wrong and the structures right, and
repairing it the other way would have moved 419 measured values onto the wrong
molecules.

**Evidence needed:** the Materials/Methods section of PMID 37985700. Nothing is
touched until then.

*(Note: `C14-PEG2000` on `BL_2023_3CR` is a legitimate alias — DMG is
dimyristoyl glycerol, i.e. C14 — not a third contradiction.)*

**Verified by me as LIVE at HEAD** in `curation/lin_aromatic_2026/digitize_fig2fgh.py`.

```python
worst = max((abs(d) for p in qc.values() if "error" not in p ...), default=nan)
```

in the very file whose round-2 comment says "48 printed numbers were being
compared and the result was printed, never checked".

**Proposed repair (not applied):** `assert not [p for p in qc.values() if
"error" in p]` and `assert len(out_rows) == sum(len(c) for ... in PANELS)`.

## F-054 (OPEN, corpus-wide) — the excipient ratio mapping: cholesterol 46.5 or 16?

**Raised by** the `whiteheadka_30637934` curation, 2026-11-10. **Verified by me
against `data/` the same day, and the corpus is sharper than the report.**

`curation/whiteheadka_30637934/source/` SI p.2 reads, in prose order:

> "lipidoid, cholesterol, ...DOPE..., C14-PEG2000 ... **35 : 16 : 46.5 : 2.5**"

Read positionally that assigns **cholesterol 16** and **DOPE 46.5**. The corpus
does the opposite, with no exceptions anywhere:

| | |
|---|---|
| rows with `CHL_molratio` == 46.5 | **13,433** |
| rows with `CHL_molratio` == 16 | **0** |
| rows with `HL_molratio` == 16 | **13,448** |
| rows with `HL_molratio` == 46.5 | **0** |
| experiments at `IL=35` carrying a 46.5 | **18**, every one `IL 35 / CHL 46.5 / HL 16 / PEG 2.5` |

The 18: `AC_2025`, `BL_2023`, `BL_2023_3CR`, `BL_2024`, `FG_2026`, `JC_2023`,
`JW_2024`, `LM_2019`, `LR_2023`, `LX_2024`, `LX_2024_2`, `LX_2024_3`, `MO_2026`,
`NC_2024`, `QL_2026`, `YX_2024`, `YX_2026`, `YX_2026_SDL`.

So this is **not a drift between curations** — the corpus is 100% internally
uniform across 18 experiments and 13,433 rows. Exactly one of two things is
true:

1. the corpus convention is right and this SI's prose order does not match its
   own numbers (publishers' formulation sentences frequently do not), or
2. **13,433 rows carry cholesterol and the helper phospholipid swapped.**

**Not resolvable from the corpus, because the corpus cannot disagree with
itself.** It needs a primary source that states a mol% next to a named lipid
unambiguously — a Materials sentence of the form "cholesterol (46.5 mol%)", an
SI table with one lipid per row, or a vendor formulation sheet. `BL_2023_3CR`
uses the *identical* printed excipient name `C14-PEG2000` with DOPE 16 /
cholesterol 46.5, and this lab's own 2024 paper (staged as `whiteheadka_38888972`)
records the order "35 : 46.5 : 16 : 2.5" — i.e. the same numbers in the other
prose order, which is evidence the orders are not stable between papers and
positional reading is unsafe.

**Why it matters, and why it does NOT invalidate the manuscript's results.**
Results 1-5 run on `Experiment_value` and on ionizable-lipid structure; none of
them reads `CHL_molratio` or `HL_molratio`. So no reported number moves either
way. But it matters for two things: any model using **formulation features**
(the companion modelling paper does), and the §8 Methods statement about
formulation metadata. It is also exactly the class §6 warns about — a convention
asserted uniformly everywhere and therefore invisible to every internal check.

**The curation's handling was right and is the posture to keep:** it emitted on
the corpus convention and flagged all 11 rows, rather than emitting the
positional reading. Its reasoning is worth preserving verbatim — an excipient
field differing from every other experiment **for a prose-ordering reason would
manufacture precisely the source-separability signal this project exists to
measure.** A curation that "fixes" a convention in one experiment creates a
provenance fingerprint.

**Action:** resolve from a primary source before the corpus is released or any
formulation-feature model is trained. Do NOT change 13,433 rows on the strength
of one SI's prose order.

## F-055 (OPEN, affects Result 3) — the lipidoid amine catalogue is SHARED ACROSS LABS, and `IL_name` tokens carry cross-lab meaning for heads

**Raised independently by three curations on 2026-11-10** (`xuq_32520527`,
`xuq_24574196`, `xuq_32662132`), each noticing it from a different paper.
**Verified by me against `data/` directly.**

The Xu-lab (Tufts) papers number their amine heads, and so do the
Anderson/Akinc-lineage papers. **They are the same catalogue.** Measured:

| head token | structure, as drawn in the corpus | rows | experiments |
|---|---|---|---|
| `93` / `A93` | 1-(3-aminopropyl)imidazole | 16 | `AA_2008`, `KW_2014` |
| `9322` / `A9322` | **1-(3-aminopropyl)-2-methylimidazole** | 22 | `YR_2024` |
| `80` / `80_O*` | N,N-dimethyl-1,3-propanediamine | — | `AA_2008`, `KW_2014`, `QL_2026` |
| `75`, `113`, `400` | Xu catalogue heads | — | `QL_2026` |

The 3-aminopropylimidazole head alone appears on **277 in-vitro + 25 in-vivo
rows, 191 unique structures, 13 experiments** (`AA_2008`, `BL_2023`,
`BL_2023_3CR`, `BL_2024`, `JW_2024`, `KW_2014`, `KZ_2016`, `XH_2024_2`,
`YR_2024`, `YY_2023`, `ZC_2023`, plus `AP_2025`/`LR_2023` in vivo).

**Why this matters for the manuscript.** §3 says never to key on `IL_name`
across experiments, and that stands for *whole lipids* — 288 names carry
different structures in different experiments. But for **heads** the token is
meaningful across labs, and that is a different claim with a different
consequence: Result 2 states ionizable lipids are near source-exclusive, which
remains true at the level of complete molecules. **Partial chemical overlap at
the head level is much larger than the whole-molecule overlap Result 3
measures**, and a reader may ask whether that weakens "near-disjoint chemical
support". It does not change any computed number — Result 3 compares *identical
lipids* — but it should be stated rather than discovered by a referee. Candidate
sentence for §5: the supports are near-disjoint in complete molecules while
sharing combinatorial *building blocks*, which is exactly why recognising
provenance is easy and transferring structure-activity is not.

**It is also a free external check that has now paid off four times**: a
curation whose paper printed no HRMS, no formula, no MW and drew no structure
had its head identity fixed by three independent corpus attestations.

### Two things from that curation worth keeping as method notes

**Never write a benchmark SMILES from memory.** Its first three strings for
SM-102, DLin-MC3-DMA and ALC-0315 were **all wrong** — SM-102 one CH2 short of
C44H87NO5, MC3 one CH2 short on a tail, ALC-0315 with the ester reversed. All
parsed, all canonicalised, no radicals; **only the InChIKey saw it.** This is
precisely why `provenance/reagent_registry.py` exists, and it is now in every
brief. Taken from the registry, the benchmarks then hit all three corpus keys
3/3.

**Its strongest check identified an unstated metric and a published sign error.**
Fig S4b prints four correlation coefficients and never says which correlation.
Against Table S2's mol% and 58 digitised bars, **Spearman reproduces all four
magnitudes to |delta| <= 0.0160 while Pearson matches only 2 of 4** — so the
unstated metric is Spearman, and the printed DMG-PEG coefficient **has the wrong
sign**: no parameterisation of Table S2 makes it negative (mol% +0.532, rank
+0.532, block-share +0.299, ratio -0.296 at the wrong magnitude). Reported as a
contradiction, not smoothed. Its seed is also instructive: a rank-preserving
x1.10 leaves the agreement count at 4, because Spearman is correctly blind to
it, while a rank perturbation drops it to 0.

**And a new figure-furniture hazard:** Fig S4b's inset correlation table is drawn
**inside the plot box**, and its ~300 px rule crossed a bar's window, reading
**149.4% on a 0-150 axis**. Fixed with a furniture strip — *a horizontal run
longer than three bar widths is not a bar top* — not by moving a threshold. Now
in every brief.

### F-055 AMENDMENT (2026-11-10, same day) — the shared catalogue is REAL but NOT UNIVERSAL, and using it blindly reproduces the `AC_2025` failure mode

I recorded F-055 as a positive finding and put it in every subsequent brief as a
free external check. **`curation/xuq_23651337` then rejected it for its own
paper, by measurement, and it is right.** I re-derived the numbers from `data/`:

| | |
|---|---|
| distinct leading catalogue numbers in `AA_2008` + `KW_2014` | **255** |
| contiguous? | **no** |
| of 1..16, present in the catalogue | **7** (1, 6, 7, 10, 11, 13, 15) |
| absent | **2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 12, 14, 16** |

That paper's Fig S3 plots a **contiguous 1..16**. A catalogue that is
non-contiguous over 255 values cannot produce a contiguous run of 16, so those
tokens are **local indices**, not catalogue numbers. And the trap has teeth:
**the corpus would have happily supplied a structure for 7 of the 16**, putting
up to 16 measured values on wrong molecules — which is exactly `AC_2025`, where
a confident, internally consistent repair assigned 419 measurements to the wrong
molecules (F-014).

**So the brief now carries a discriminator rather than an invitation:**

> A head-number token is only a catalogue reference if the *pattern* of tokens
> is consistent with the catalogue. The Anderson/Akinc numbering is **sparse and
> non-contiguous** (255 values, large gaps). **A paper numbering its members
> 1..N contiguously is therefore using local indices**, and a corpus lookup on
> those tokens will return plausible, wrong structures for whichever happen to
> collide. Test the pattern before trusting any single hit, and prefer a
> structural attestation (retro-Michael, substructure, HRMS) over a name match.

The four places F-055 genuinely paid off all had a **structural** attestation,
not just a numeric one: `93`/`A93` resolving to one SMILES across two
experiments; `9322` confirmed by its own mass axis at an 18.9x margin; `80`
confirmed by truncating both molecules to a common ester; and this paper's own
`*_N18` family confirmed **43/43 by retro-Michaelisation** against a structure
built from the chemical name alone. The failure case had only a number.

Both halves of F-055 are now in the briefs, and the amendment is the half that
matters more, because it is the one that can silently corrupt data.

## F-057 (OPEN, touches 360 ALREADY-MERGED rows) — `18PG` is SOPG by one paper's text and DOPG by the other's drawing, for the SAME library

Raised by `curation/maohq_39375194` (Mao lab, ACS Nano 2024, PMID 39375194),
2026-11-10. **Verified against `data/` by me; the curation correctly did NOT
repair it.**

`CLAUDE.md` §3 already records one `18PG` repair: the corpus had stored it as a
symmetric 18:0/18:0 lipid, and it was corrected to **SOPG (18:0/18:1)** because
*"YZ_2022 Materials (Nat Commun 2022, PMC9310361) names it verbatim"*. That fix
is live and uniform:

| name | rows | experiments | stored structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| `18PG` | **1,474** | `WT_2026` 1,114 · `YZ_2022` 180 · `YZ_2024` 180 | one SOPG string, identical on all 1,474 |
| `DOPG` | 81 | `ZT_2026` | a different string |

**The new paper is the same lab, the same 1,080-formulation library, and it
draws `18PG` with a cis C=C in BOTH tails** — i.e. DOPG — and its deposited data
file carries `Double_bonds = 2`, which SOPG (one oleoyl) cannot be. So:

- the **Nat Commun text** names SOPG, verbatim, which is what §3's repair rests on;
- the **ACS Nano drawing** and the repo file's own descriptor say DOPG.

**If the drawing is right, 360 already-merged rows carry the wrong helper lipid
today** (`YZ_2022` 180 + `YZ_2024` 180), and the `WT_2026` 1,114 would need
checking separately since that is a different lab.

**Why nothing was changed.** This is the `AC_2025` shape exactly — a confident,
internally consistent repair that assigned **419 measurements to the wrong
molecules** (F-014) — and §6's rule is that the *direction* of a repair has to be
established before it is applied. One caution specific to this case: a
`Double_bonds` column in a one-hot file could plausibly be an **ionizable**-lipid
descriptor (MC3 has four C=C), not a helper descriptor, so it is not decisive on
its own; the curation read it as a helper feature, which is likely but unproven.
It emitted as drawn with `confidence = medium` on 972 rows and left the corpus
alone. That is the right call.

**Resolution needs a primary source that names a structure unambiguously** — an
Avanti catalogue number for the "18PG" the lab bought, an SI Materials line with
a full chemical name, or an author query. Note this is **error class 48**
recurring on the same reagent: a shorthand expanded to a specific structure,
where the expansion is the thing in doubt. `provenance/reagent_registry.py`'s
`18PG` entry currently *asserts* SOPG and C11 certifies it, so **if DOPG is
right, the registry is actively enforcing the wrong answer on 1,474 rows** —
which is why this cannot be left as a note in a curation folder.

**Action:** resolve from a catalogue number or an author query before release,
and do not change 1,474 rows on the strength of a drawing.

Raised by `curation/dahlmanje_30275336` (Sago/Dahlman, PNAS 2018), 2026-11-10.
**Verified against `data/` by me:**

| pool | `Experiment_batching` | rows | experiments |
|---|---|---|---|
| vivo | `individual` | 1,396 | 19 |
| vivo | **`barcoded`** | **407** | **1 — `LR_2023`** |
| vitro | `individual` | 20,924 | 37 |
| vitro | **(blank)** | **8,271** | **6** |

So `LR_2023` is **407 of 1,803 in-vivo rows, 22.6% of the pool**, and it is the
only barcoded experiment in the corpus.

**Why the curation's framing is the useful part.** It declined to refuse pooled
data on principle — correctly, since the schema has an
`Experiment_batching` vocabulary of `{barcoded, individual}` and `LR_2023` is
already merged and *declared* as barcoded, so a blanket refusal would contradict
the project's own precedent. What it established instead, by reconstructing the
paper's own worked example, is that the readout is **compositional**:

```
nd_i = [(count_i / Σcount) / (input_i / Σinput)] / Σ_k[same] × 100 %
```

reproducing **6/6 column totals, 18/18 cells, 3/3 input shares, 18/18 cells,
residual 0.0** — and the authors **drew** `Total (%) = 100` under both tables
without ever computing it, which the unrounded reconstruction hits at exactly
100.000000.

**The consequence for us is specific, not vague.** Each value is that LNP's
**share of its own pool**. Its three screens used pools of 71 / 78 / 106
members, so their values live on three different simplices — a compositional
value is not a property of the molecule alone, it is a property of the molecule
*and the other members of its pool*. `Experiment_value` is a per-experiment
z-score of log10, so for a barcoded experiment **we are z-scoring a share**.
Two things follow:

1. **Result 3** compares within-source normalised ranks of identical lipids
   across sources. A rank *within* a compositional pool is better behaved than
   the raw share — ranking is invariant to the common denominator — so the
   damage is limited. But the in-vivo concordance is already LOW-CONFIDENCE at
   n=9 (T8), and 22.6% of that pool now has a second caveat attached.
2. **Any absolute or cross-source comparison** of those 407 values is comparing
   positions on different simplices.

**Also unresolved and separate: 8,271 vitro rows across 6 experiments have a
BLANK `Experiment_batching`.** Per §3, a blank is "not stated", not "clean", so
we do not currently know whether any of those are pooled. That is 28% of the
in-vitro pool whose batching is simply unrecorded, and it should be filled in
from the source papers before Methods describes the corpus.

**Action:** decide whether `LR_2023`'s 407 rows belong in Stage 5's in-vivo
concordance, and state the compositional caveat in Methods either way. Fill in
the 8,271 blank batching values. Neither changes a frozen number today —
`results/` was computed on the old 34/17 corpus — but both have to be settled
before the pipeline is re-run.

## F-066 (sharpens F-060) — the corpus precedent for pooled data requires a TRANSLATED readout, which this paper's does not have

- **a large block of rows whose variance is formulation, not chemistry** — and
  `Experiment_value` is a per-experiment z-score, so those rows would be

  chemistry — the pool has exactly two today (MC3, SM-102) — and unlike those
  two, cKK-E12 is **not** a universal benchmark.

1. **Exclude one-lipid screens.** Cleanest for Results 1-5; loses the cKK-E12
   in-vivo shared point and 74% of this run's rows.
2. **Admit them, flagged**, with a schema column recording that the experiment's
   variance is formulation (`design_axis = formulation | chemistry`). Keeps the
   shared point; requires every downstream analysis to decide whether to filter,

3. **Admit only the individually-measured validation arms** (typically 5-50

**Recommendation: option 3 as the default, with option 2 available by explicit

the pool otherwise lacks — i.e. the F-064 case, and only on the in-vivo side
where shared chemistry is down to two structures. Either way the rule belongs in

### F-070 AMENDMENT — a positive control needs a positive control, and a non-zero count is not one

## F-061 RESOLVED (probably not a corpus error) — the two "200" series are DIFFERENT AMINES sharing a token, and each is internally self-consistent

`curation/andersondg_26469188` supplied the derivation; **I verified it against
`data/` and it is exact.** Within a series that holds the amine fixed and walks
the tail, **dC/d(tail) equals the number of arms**:

| series | tails | carbons | dC/d(tail) | implies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `KW_2014` `A200_C10…C14` | 10,11,12,13,14 | 75, 80, 85, 90, 95 | **5.0, 5.0, 5.0, 5.0** | **5 arms** |
| `KL_2010` `200C8…C14` | 8,10,12,14 | 20, 22, 24, 26 | **1.0, 1.0, 1.0** | **1 arm** |

Both ladders are perfectly linear with zero residual, so **neither is a
corrupted copy of the other** — each is a coherent homologous series. That alone
retires "one of them is a typo".

**And the nitrogen count settles what they are.** `KL_2010`'s `200C12` is
**C24H51N3O — three N**; `AC_2025`'s `C12-200` is **C70H145N5O5 — five N**. A
three-nitrogen amine cannot carry five arms, so these are **not two
substitution levels of one amine**: they are **two different amine cores both
labelled 200**.

So F-061 is most likely **not a data error at all** — it is F-055's amendment
in its sharpest form: *the token is not a universal catalogue reference*, and
two labs used "200" for different starting amines. The naming order even differs
(`200C12` vs `C12-200`), which is the usual signal of two conventions meeting.

**Still not repaired, and should not be**, for two reasons. First, the
alternative remains open: Love 2010 reported mono-, di- and poly-substituted
lipidoids as distinct products, so a 1-arm `200C12` could be a legitimate
mono-adduct of the *same* amine — except the N count argues against it, and I
have not read that paper. Second, this is the `AC_2025` shape, where a confident
repair put 419 measurements on the wrong molecules. **Resolution needs the Love
2010 SI**, not a corpus inference. Downgraded from "possible corpus error on 4
rows" to "**a naming collision to document in Methods**", which is a materially
different and much cheaper outcome.

Measured for the same note: `IL_name contains "200"` returns **526 rows, 24
names, 24 distinct structures, ≥3 incompatible chemotypes**. Never join on it.

## F-049 SHARPENED AND MEASURED — `JW_2024`'s PEG name disagrees with 8 experiments, and the evidence says the NAME is wrong, not the structure

Raised long ago as an open item; `curation/goulart_41138890` measured it, and I
re-derived it from `data/`. The picture is now precise:

| `PEG_name` | structure | rows | experiments |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DMPE-PEG2000` | **C122H242NO53P** — phosphoethanolamine (has N **and** P) | **3,611** | 8 (`BL_2023`, `BL_2024`, `JC_2023`, `LM_2019`, `LR_2023`, `LX_2024`, `LZ_2025`, `NC_2024`) |
| `DMPE-PEG2000` | **C122H242O50** — glycerol ether (no N, no P) | **1,873** | **1 — `JW_2024`** |
| `DMG-PEG2000` | C122H242O50 — glycerol ether | **22,973** | **41** |
| `C14-PEG2000` | C122H242O50 — glycerol ether | 480 | 1 (`BL_2023_3CR`) |

**The chemistry decides which half is wrong.** DM**PE** is dimyristoyl-
**phosphoethanolamine**, which by its name must contain N and P; DM**G** is
dimyristoyl-**glycerol**, which must not. `JW_2024`'s 1,873 rows carry the
**glycerol ether** under the **phosphoethanolamine name** — and that structure is
exactly what **41 experiments call `DMG-PEG2000`**.

So, applying the `AC_2025` lesson (*establish which half is wrong before
repairing*): eight experiments and the reagent's own chemical name agree that
`DMPE-PEG2000` means the phosphoethanolamine, and `JW_2024`'s **structure**
matches 22,973 rows of `DMG-PEG2000`. **The weight of evidence is that
`JW_2024`'s NAME is wrong and its structure is right** — the opposite of the
`18PG` case (F-057), and the same direction as `AC_2025` itself, where the names
were wrong and the values belonged to the structures.

**Still not repaired.** It needs `JW_2024`'s source paper (PMID 37985700) to say
which PEG-lipid was actually used. If the paper says DMG, the fix is a one-column
name change on 1,873 rows and nothing moves. If it says DMPE, then 1,873 rows
carry the wrong *structure* and the repair is the expensive direction. **Do not
guess from the corpus alone** — that is exactly how 419 measurements once landed
on the wrong molecules.

**And this is the second measured case of the hazard `corpus_components.smi()`
cannot see** (after `C14-PEG2000` in F-060). That curation found its paper draws
`C14PEG2k` as a **phosphoethanolamine-carbamate-mPEG45** while the corpus name
`C14-PEG2000` resolves **unambiguously** to the glycerol ether — so `smi()` would
**not raise** and would hand back the wrong molecule. It left `PEG_SMILES` blank,
which is the right call and matches §3's standing posture that a PEG block length
is a distribution and no single SMILES is right.
