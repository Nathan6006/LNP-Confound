# xd_2026_lipidai — Duan X et al., *Acta Pharm Sin B* 2026, PMID 42592089

DOI 10.1016/j.apsb.2025.10.040 · repo github.com/yuhanchenzz/LipidAI (MIT) cloned to `repo/`

## What was merged, and what was not

The repo holds **two things that must not be confused**.

| | rows | merged? |
|---|---|---|
| the paper's own in-vivo screen (Fig 5) | 12 | **yes** → `XD_2026` in `data/data_vivo.csv` |
| `LipidDB-Ionizable lipid Library.xlsx` Sheet1 | 485 | **no** |
| its `sheet1+tail` / `sheet1-tail` (MTA augmentation) | 720 | **no** |

**LipidDB is a literature compilation, not an experiment.** Its luminescence values
were lifted from the **47 studies** the paper names (§2.1.1), each on its own dose,
route, timepoint and instrument scale. Giving it one `Experiment_ID` would fabricate
a lab and corrupt the source key Results 1–4 rest on. Three further facts make the
pooling non-recoverable rather than merely untidy:

- `descriptors_1107.csv` — the file the model actually trains on — carries an
  `inject_way` feature with **four** levels (961 / 85 / 50 / 11), so at least four
  administration routes are pooled into the single "Total expression" column. That
  feature appears nowhere in the released xlsx, so the xlsx is not the whole dataset.
- **7 of the 24 duplicate structures carry conflicting class labels**, with value
  disagreements up to five orders of magnitude — same molecule, different source
  paper, inside one compiled database.
- One paper (Lu/Percec, *Pharmaceutics* 2023) supplies **124 of the 485 rows**; the
  top 10 supply 318.

It is kept here because it is useful *as* a second, independent literature-pooled
corpus: external evidence for Results 1/2/5, a ready-made cross-lab discordance
datapoint in those 7 conflicts, and 83 lipids shared with our corpus.

**The 720 MTA rows are synthetic** — structures generated by adding or removing one
methyl, carrying the parent's label. Not measurements. 121 of them are identical to
a lipid already in the original 485 (label-duplicated augmentation in the source
model).

## XD_2026 — the 12 merged rows

BALB/c male 6–8 wk, tail-vein IV, whole-body IVIS luciferase at 6 h, p/sec/cm²/sr.
IL : DSPC : cholesterol : DMG-PEG2000 = 50 : 10 : 38.5 : 1.5 mol%, microfluidic,
0.01 M citrate pH 3 aqueous, dialysed into 0.01 M citrate pH 6.
mRNA dose and N:P are **absent from the paper** and are left blank, not imputed.

`Experiment_value` = z(log₁₀(raw)), population sd, over these 12 rows.

### Structures were traced, not counted by eye

`stage02_trace.py` binarises Fig 5A, skeletonises, traces each stroke, simplifies it
with Douglas–Peucker and counts vertices, writing an annotated overlay per structure
(`00_pages/png/ov_*.png`) so every bond is checkable. ±1 carbon is this project's
documented failure mode and eyes are what fail at it.

**The benchmark control found a real error in the paper.** Run on the three
benchmarks drawn in the same panel:

| | traced from Fig 5A | corpus | |
|---|---|---|---|
| SM-102 | exact | exact | ✅ |
| DLin-MC3-DMA | exact | exact | ✅ |
| ALC-0315 | `HO-(CH2)3-N` | `HO-(CH2)4-N` | ❌ paper draws it one carbon short |

The decisive control is internal: **E1's head, drawn in the same style 390 px below,
has four vertices.** So the drawing program can and does draw a 4-carbon hydroxyalkyl
— ALC-0315's head is a localised authoring error, not a convention.

Consequences, both deliberate:
- the three benchmarks are emitted with their **corpus** SMILES;
- **E1–E9 carry `confidence = medium`**, because they come from a figure with a
  demonstrated 1-in-3 ±1-carbon error rate on its own controls. There is no other
  source: the paper says E1–E9 were "provided by WestGene Biopharmaceutical" and
  gives no name, formula, HRMS, synthesis or SI. `reference_structures.csv` carries
  an empty row for each so the **absence** of an anchor is visible, and C10 records
  the ALC-0315 override as `expected_mismatch_paper_drawing_is_wrong` rather than as
  a prose note.

### Values came from two figures that agree

Fig 5C is a log-axis bar chart, digitised against the 10⁶–10⁹ major ticks (tick
residuals < 0.002 decades). Fig 5D independently assigns each lipid an A/B/C class
with thresholds printed in both the paper and the xlsx header (A > 5×10⁷,
B 5×10⁶–5×10⁷, C < 5×10⁶). **All 12 digitised values fall in their own Fig 5D band**
— two figures agreeing is what makes the digitisation trustworthy, not the fit alone.
Fig 5D also reproduces the two numbers the text prints: 9/12 = 75 % overall, and
class B 1/3 correct.

### Fields that are readings, not printed facts

Recorded in `07_qc.json` under `assumptions`, not silently:
`Model_target='whole_body'` (Methods 2.3.4 describes both whole-animal imaging and
ex-vivo organ measurement; the caption says neither), `Route='intravenous'` (from the
Fig 5 caption and body text, not Methods), and `Dialysis_buffer='citrate'` — faithful
to Methods but a category no other source in the corpus carries.

## Known defects *in the published dataset* (not ours)

- Table 1 vs xlsx: **3 luminescence disagreements**, two of which are also **class**
  disagreements (row 4: B/3.16e7 vs C/9.0e3; row 5: A/5.8e7 vs B/6.62e6; row 15:
  2.0e8 vs 2.0e10, both A). The class is the model's training label.
- Table 1 row 4 also cites a different source paper than the xlsx assigns.
- The paper states 475 lipid samples; the xlsx has 485 rows. **Unexplained** — no
  combination of 485 / 461 unique / 414 valued / 484 unique raw strings gives 475.
  485 + 720 = 1205 ≠ the stated 1107 augmented points either.
