Inclusion criteria — what had to be true to stay on this list

A row is a candidate data source if it clears two tests: (1) per-LNP quantified delivery of a nucleic acid — mRNA, siRNA or pDNA, all three of which the corpus already contains (Cargo = mRNA 14,195 rows, siRNA 3,641, pDNA 1,192) — read out as expression, knockdown, editing or barcode abundance; (2) not already in the corpus, re-verified by PMID, DOI and ≥85% title similarity (0 of 74 matched).

Only 7 papers were struck, all for failing test 1: protein and Cas9-RNP cargoes, two PEG-lipid libraries that hold the ionizable lipid constant, a patent set with structures but no activity values, and one nasal-spray paper a keyword sweep caught by mistake.

Rather than exclude an experiment for being a different kind of experiment, every row carries two labels you can filter on. Varies is what the library sweeps — IL structure, formulation, or both. Measures is what the assay quantifies. Nothing is dropped on either axis, so the heterogeneity stays visible at analysis time instead of being buried in a curation decision.

Two earlier exclusions were reversed on review, and both were wrong in the same way — they applied a criterion this corpus does not follow. Formulation sweeps belong. AC_2025 ("comet_lance") is 1,978 rows on seven unique ILs — 283 rows per IL, the highest ratio in the corpus — and YZ_2022 / YZ_2024 are 1,080 rows on one IL each. A rule excluding new formulation sweeps would exclude the corpus's own largest ones. Barcode screens belong too. Barcodes are nucleic acids, the corpus already pools five different Experiment_method quantities, and the paper's central claim is that quantities do not rank-correlate across environments — so a deliberately different readout is evidence, not contamination. The one caveat worth carrying forward: pooled barcode screens co-administer every LNP in a single animal, so each value is competitive and within-pool rather than independent. That is a reason to treat them as their own environment, which the measures label now lets you do.

How to read this

Priority 1 = add first. Type distinguishes what the data actually is: in-vitro IL library (per-lipid transfection, directly poolable into your corpus), in-vivo barcoded (pooled DNA/mRNA-barcode delivery — a different readout, not rank-comparable to your in-vitro pool), formulation space (few ionizable lipids, many ratio/helper variants — large row counts but almost no IL-structure signal), compiled dataset (someone else's pooled multi-study corpus — matters for positioning, not as raw data), and platform/method (little usable SAR).

Library sizes were auto-extracted from abstracts, then hand-verified for every Priority 1–2 row. Where the abstract does not state a size, the field is blank and the note says so — those need an SI check.

Data access grades what can actually be obtained, not what the paper claims to have measured. A = a machine-readable file is reachable right now. B = the SI document is reachable; extraction is manual. C = nothing reachable from this machine — no PMC deposit, or the publisher blocks automated fetching (nature.com, pnas.org and pubs.acs.org all do). C is not a rejection: CWRU library access will likely work. A fourth grade, confirmed unobtainable, is not shown — those rows are deleted from the dataset rather than displayed, and the builder's REJECTED set re-drops them if a later sweep finds them again. One paper is currently in it (Ardekani, PMID 40267508: data curated but never published, authors declined on request). Where a data-availability statement could be read, "available upon reasonable request" was treated as a downgrade to C, because it carries real refusal risk.

Three findings that matter more than any single dataset

1 · A correction to my earlier recommendation. The Mitchell 500-lipid "plug-and-play" paper is already in your corpus as XH_2025 — LNPDB curated it from the bioRxiv preprint (PMID 40060499), and PNAS 2026 (PMID 42190011) is the same data. It was my top pick last round; it is not addable. Both rows are kept below so the duplication is visible.

2 · Your corpus is one academic lineage. AA_2008 (Akinc), KL_2010 (Love), KW_2014 (Whitehead), JM_2016 (Miller), KZ_2016 (Zhou), LM_2019 (Miao), SL_2020/21, JW_2024 (Jiang), BL_2023/2024 (Li) are all Anderson-lab papers; YX_2024, YX_2026, YX_2026_SDL, MO_2026, FG_2026 are all Bowen Li, an Anderson alum. More than half your in-vitro sources descend from one lineage — and cross-lab concordance is still 0.083. That makes Result 3 markedly stronger at zero curation cost.

3 · Independent published confirmation of your thesis. Adzavon et al., Int J Pharm 2026 pooled 613 LNP formulations from 18 studies, ran nested formulation-grouped cross-validation to remove repeated-measures leakage, and found "no reliable predictive validity in any of the six adequately powered studies." Different endpoint (storage stability), identical methodology and identical conclusion. Meanwhile arXiv 2609.17721 (Sep 2026) pools 476 formulations from 81 studies and reports ROC-AUC 0.84–0.87 with no cross-study validation at all. One paper confirms you; the other is the live failure mode.
